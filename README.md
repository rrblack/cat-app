# Meow Translator 🐱

An Android app that listens to your cat and translates its meows (and purrs, trills, hisses, growls, yowls
and chatters) into what it is most likely trying to say, based on published cat-vocalization research and
a model trained on real recorded meows.

> “Food, please!” · 🍽️ Food request · Moderate confidence · Meow

Everything runs on the phone. No account, no network, and recordings never leave the device.

**English and 日本語.** The whole app (screens, translations, explanations and history) is available in
English and Japanese. It follows the phone's language by default; switch any time with the 日本語 / English
button on the Translate screen or under *My cat → Language*. On Android 13+ it also appears in the system's
per-app language settings. Japanese cats speak casual Japanese (「ごはんちょうだい！」「おかえりー！会いたかったにゃ！」).

## How accurate is it?

Cats don't speak in words, so no app can produce a literal translation. What science *can* do is tell
situations and emotional states apart from the acoustics of a call. This app does that as well as the
available data allows, and says how sure it is.

All numbers below are measured on cats the model had **never heard** (leave-one-cat-out cross-validation
over the 21 cats in the CatMeows dataset). They are regenerated by the trainer and shown in the app:

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| Food vs. brushing vs. isolation meows, general model | ~48% balanced accuracy (chance = 33%) |
| Same, when the model has heard other meows from the same cat | ~61% |
| After the owner teaches the app 3 / 10 / 15 meows | ~53% / ~59% / ~60% |
| Meows recognised as meows by the call-type rules | 434 / 440 (99%) |

Before anything is translated, a **cat / person / other detector** throws out sounds that aren't the cat.
Measured per whole recording, on speakers, cats and sound clips it never heard:

| Recording | Correctly handled |
|---|---|
| People talking (LibriSpeech, 40 speakers) | 97% ignored |
| Household and outdoor sounds (ESC-50) | 86% ignored |
| Laughing, coughing, a crying baby… (ESC-50) | 65% ignored |
| Cat meows (CatMeows, unseen cats) | 96% translated |
| Cat recordings from other equipment (ESC-50 cats) | 95% translated |

Meows turn out to be very individual (the same finding as the research literature), which is why the app
asks *“Was this right?”* after each meow and learns your particular cat's way of meowing.

## Features

- **Translate**: tap the mic near your cat; recording stops automatically when the cat goes quiet. You can
  also translate an existing audio file (WAV, M4A, MP3, …).
- **Result**: a first-person phrase, the likely intent with alternatives, mood (unhappy↔happy,
  calm↔excited), the call type, a pitch-melody chart, and a plain-language “why” with sources.
- **Teach**: label what was really happening; a per-cat model learns from it and gets more weight as you
  add examples. Anything that wasn't your cat can be removed with *Not my cat*.
- **Ignores people and noise**: talking, the TV, keyboards, doors and the like are filtered out instead of
  being "translated", and are never saved to history.
- **History**: replay past recordings and re-translate them with everything learned since.
- **My cat**: name, age group and neuter status (these change how e.g. yowling is read), and the app language.
- **How it works**: the method, the honest accuracy numbers, and all references.

## How it works

```
audio ─► resample to 8 kHz ─► frame analysis (10 ms hop)
          │                     ├─ YIN pitch candidates + Viterbi tracking (no octave jumps)
          │                     ├─ loudness, spectral centroid/flatness/roll-off, band energies
          │                     └─ 13 MFCCs
          ├─► call segmentation (adaptive noise floor, hysteresis, echo-tail trimming)
          ├─► cat / person / other detector ── drops people talking and household noise
          ├─► per-call features: duration, melody shape & slope, pitch range, jitter,
          │   voice roughness, amplitude-modulation rate (purr pulsing), MFCC stats
          ├─► call type   ── literature-based acoustic rules (meow/trill/purr/hiss/growl/yowl/chatter)
          ├─► meow context ── softmax regression trained on CatMeows (food / brushing / isolation)
          ├─► prosody cues ── rising vs falling melody, length, roughness (Schötz et al.)
          ├─► your cat    ── k-nearest-neighbours on meows you labelled
          └─► translation: intent, mood, confidence, phrase, evidence
```

The intent shown is a probability mix over all of these, so an ambiguous sound gives an honest
low-confidence answer instead of a made-up certain one.

## Project layout

| Module | What |
|---|---|
| `core/` | Pure Kotlin/JVM engine: DSP, features, classifiers, translator, and English/Japanese texts (`i18n/`). Unit-tested with synthetic calls. |
| `trainer/` | Offline tool that trains the meow-context model on CatMeows and regenerates `ContextModelWeights.kt`. |
| `app/` | Android app (Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3, minSdk 26). |

## Getting the app

**Download:** every push builds the APKs on GitHub Actions. Open the latest *Build* run, download the
`meow-translator-apk` artifact, unzip it, and install `app-release.apk` on your phone (allow installing
from unknown sources).

**Build it yourself** (JDK 17+ and the Android SDK, or just open the folder in Android Studio):

```bash
./gradlew :core:test            # engine unit tests
./gradlew :app:assembleRelease  # app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk
```

## Retraining the model

```bash
mkdir -p data && cd data
curl -L -o dataset.zip https://zenodo.org/api/records/4008297/files/dataset.zip/content
unzip dataset.zip && cd ..
./gradlew :trainer:run --args="data/dataset core/src/main/kotlin/com/rrblack/meowtranslator/core/classify/ContextModelWeights.kt"
```

The trainer prints cross-validated accuracy, the confusion matrix, and the simulated personalisation
curve, and writes them into the generated file so the app shows the current numbers.

The cat / person / other detector (`TrainCallDetector`) additionally needs LibriSpeech dev-clean
(https://www.openslr.org/12) and ESC-50 (https://github.com/karolpiczak/ESC-50), converted to 8 kHz mono WAV
(e.g. `ffmpeg -i in.flac -t 4 -ac 1 -ar 8000 out.wav`; speech files named `S_<speaker>_<id>.wav`):

```bash
./gradlew :trainer:installDist
java -cp "trainer/build/install/trainer/lib/*" com.rrblack.meowtranslator.trainer.TrainCallDetectorKt \
  data/dataset data/other_vocalizations data/speech8k data/esc8k data/esc50.csv \
  core/src/main/kotlin/com/rrblack/meowtranslator/core/classify/CallDetectorWeights.kt
```

`trainer` also has `EvaluateRobustness` (meows replayed through simulated room echo / noise),
`EvaluateNonCat` (what the app says about non-cat sounds), `CheckVocalTypes` and `DumpFeatures`.
